\begin{abstract}
Histopathology patches have no canonical upright orientation, and the
diagnostic and tissue labels considered here are unchanged by global in-plane
rotation. Expert annotations remain costly and substantially less abundant
than unlabeled image regions. This study evaluates whether continuous rotation equivariance improves
the representations learned by a variational autoencoder (VAE) and whether
those representations are useful for subsequent classification. A conventional
convolutional VAE and a continuous-$\SOtwo$-equivariant VAE were trained on the
same 300,000 ovarian-cancer patches from 322 whole-slide images (WSIs), using
the same denoising objective, latent shape and budget of 60,000 updates. Their
checkpoints were then frozen. Evaluation used an independent cohort of 152
WSIs divided into 106 training, 23 validation and 23 test slides for supervised
tasks. The test set contained 67,138 patches for reconstruction, 23 complete
bags for five-class WSI diagnosis and 31,572 annotated patches for three-class
tissue recognition. The conventional VAE obtained a lower mean absolute
reconstruction error, although the paired WSI-bootstrap interval for the
primary difference included zero. For WSI diagnosis, the equivariant
representation obtained higher macro-F1 (0.483 versus 0.389) and accuracy
(0.609 versus 0.522), but the corresponding intervals did not establish a
difference. For tissue recognition, it produced higher macro-F1 at four of
five label budgets; only the 500-label-per-class comparison had a simultaneous
95\% interval excluding zero, and the largest budget favored the conventional
representation. The primary analysis did not establish a reconstruction
difference between the two implemented VAEs or a general classification
advantage. The WSI macro-F1 interval and four of the five simultaneous tissue
macro-F1 intervals included zero.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Digital histopathology converts glass slides into whole-slide images whose
dimensions commonly reach gigapixel scale. These images contain many local
tissue regions, but their diagnostic labels and detailed annotations require
specialist review. Representation learning from unlabeled patches offers a way
to use this image volume before fitting a smaller supervised model
\cite{litjens_survey_2017,song_artificial_2023,tellez_neural_2021}.

Histopathological images also differ from many natural-image datasets because
local tissue structures do not have a fixed upright orientation. A tissue
pattern can appear at any in-plane angle without changing its biological
meaning. Conventional convolutional networks account for this variation
mainly through rotated examples or by learning separate filters for different
orientations. Rotation-equivariant networks instead constrain intermediate
representations to transform according to the same rotation applied to the
input \cite{cohen_group_2016,cohen_steerable_2017,weiler_general_2019}.
This construction is relevant to histopathology, where rotation-equivariant
classifiers have previously been evaluated for patch-level prediction
\cite{veeling_rotation_2018}.

An equivariant architecture does not, however, ensure that a trained model
will reconstruct images more accurately or provide a better representation
for every supervised task. The effect must be measured under a common data and
training protocol. In this study, two denoising VAEs
\cite{kingma_auto_encoding_2014} are compared: a
conventional residual convolutional model and a model whose convolutional
kernels are constrained to be equivariant to the continuous planar rotation
group $\SOtwo$. Both models produce a spatial Gaussian latent representation
and are frozen before any diagnostic or tissue label is used.

The experiment addresses two questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Does the rotation-equivariant VAE improve reconstruction of unseen
    histopathology patches relative to the conventional VAE?
    \item Does the frozen equivariant representation improve classification at
    WSI level or tissue-patch level under the same supervised protocol?
\end{enumerate}

The work consists of an experimental characterization of the two VAEs and an
evaluation of their contribution to two downstream classification tasks. The
comparison preserves the image populations, spatial latent dimensions,
reconstruction objective, optimization budget and supervised data assignments.
Results are reported with uncertainty at WSI level because patches from one
slide are not independent biological observations.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Representation Learning in Histopathology}

Whole-slide images cannot generally be processed at native resolution in one
forward pass. A common approach divides each WSI into patches, learns a local
representation, and aggregates patch features for slide-level prediction.
Tellez \emph{et al.} used neural image compression to learn patch codes before
gigapixel prediction \cite{tellez_neural_2021}. Attention-based multiple
instance learning (MIL) provides a related supervised formulation in which a
WSI is treated as a bag of instances and only the bag label is required
\cite{ilse_attention_2018}. Subsequent work has applied weakly supervised MIL
to large histopathology cohorts \cite{lu_data_2021}.

The present study follows this two-stage organization. The VAE learns from
unlabeled patches. Its posterior mean is then used either as a patch-level
input for tissue recognition or as the instance representation of a WSI bag.
The VAE weights remain fixed in both cases.

\subsection{Rotation Equivariance}

For an input transformation $T_g$, a representation map $E$ is equivariant
when there is a corresponding latent action $\rho_g$ such that

\begin{equation}
E(T_g\mathbf{x})=\rho_g E(\mathbf{x}).
\end{equation}

Group-equivariant convolutional networks construct layers that satisfy this
relation for a selected transformation group \cite{cohen_group_2016}.
Steerable convolutions extend the construction to continuous rotations by
restricting the kernel space according to the representations carried by the
input and output fields \cite{cohen_steerable_2017,weiler_general_2019}.
When the target function follows the same symmetry, this restriction can
reduce the effective hypothesis space \cite{elesedy_provably_2021}. It also
changes parameter count, computation and optimization, so an empirical
comparison does not isolate symmetry unless these factors are separately
controlled.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Experimental Design}

The experiment had two stages. First, the conventional and equivariant VAEs
were trained with unlabeled patches. Second, the final checkpoints were frozen
and their deterministic posterior means were used for reconstruction and
supervised classification. No downstream label was used to update either VAE.

Both VAEs received the same ordered patches, normalization, corruption policy,
loss, number of updates and validation schedule. The supervised comparisons
also used the same WSI assignments, patch coordinates, classifier
initialization, epoch order and model-selection rule for the two
representations. The experiment therefore compares two complete frozen
representations under a common protocol; it does not estimate the isolated
causal effect of equivariance.

\subsection{Data Source and Image Units}

The data were obtained from the UBC-OCEAN ovarian-cancer resource
\cite{ubc_ocean_2023}. The diagnostic labels are clear cell carcinoma (CC),
endometrioid carcinoma (EC), high-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC), low-grade
serous carcinoma (LGSC), and mucinous carcinoma (MC). Tissue microarrays were
excluded because their scale and layout differ from conventional WSIs.

Each WSI was represented by non-overlapping RGB patches of
$256\times256$ pixels. The biological unit used to separate data populations
was the WSI, not the patch. Patches from one slide were never assigned to
different train, validation or test subsets.

\subsection{Patch Selection and Tissue Annotations}

Foreground selection for reconstruction and WSI diagnosis was derived from
the image rather than from human masks. Each distributed WSI thumbnail was
converted to HSV, and Otsu's threshold was applied to the saturation channel
\cite{otsu_threshold_1979}. A full-resolution grid cell was retained when more
than 60\% of its projected thumbnail area belonged to foreground. This
procedure prevented the supplemental tissue annotations from determining the
patches used for the primary reconstruction and diagnostic tasks.

Supplemental masks were used only to construct the tissue-recognition
population. Red, green and blue mask regions were interpreted as tumor,
stroma and necrosis. The masks are not exhaustive; an unpainted region cannot
be treated as a negative class. A patch was therefore eligible for tissue
classification only when at least 10\% of its area was annotated and at least
95\% of that annotated area belonged to one tissue class. The resulting task
classifies high-purity annotated regions and does not constitute dense
pixel-level segmentation of the complete WSI.

\subsection{Data Populations and Partitions}

Two non-overlapping WSI populations were used. VAE development used 322 WSIs
for training and 39 for validation, represented by 300,000 and 30,000 patches,
respectively. The later evaluation cohort contained 152 different WSIs and was
fixed as 106 supervised-training, 23 validation and 23 test slides before VAE
or classifier outputs on that cohort were inspected.

The class counts for train/validation/test were 23/5/5 CC, 24/6/6 EC,
40/8/8 HGSC, 11/2/2 LGSC and 8/2/2 MC. The assignment was shared by both
downstream tasks. It used diagnosis and the availability of annotated tissue
to maintain a feasible rare-tissue evaluation, but it did not use image
pixels, VAE representations or model predictions. Figure~\ref{fig:data-split}
shows the complete experimental partition.

\subsection{Conventional Variational Autoencoder}

The conventional model was derived from a residual convolutional design
informed by ResNet-18 \cite{he_deep_2016}, with the downsampling schedule
limited to preserve a spatial latent map. Its encoder used stages with 32, 48,
64 and 96 channels at spatial resolutions 256, 128, 64 and 32. Each stage
contained two residual blocks. Fixed anti-aliased downsampling was applied
between encoder stages. The decoder reversed the schedule through bilinear
upsampling followed by convolution.

Ordinary learned convolutions, GroupNorm and learned scalar gates were used
throughout the hidden layers. Separate $5\times5$ heads produced the posterior
mean and log-variance

\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mu},\log\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{2}
\in\mathbb{R}^{16\times32\times32}.
\end{equation}

The decoder received a sampled latent tensor with the same shape and returned
a raw three-channel image without a final $\tanh$ operation. The conventional
model contained 3,958,435 learned parameters.

\subsection{Continuous-$\SOtwo$-Equivariant Variational Autoencoder}

The equivariant VAE used the same input, output, spatial schedule, number of
residual blocks and posterior shape. Ordinary convolutional kernels were
replaced by steerable kernels constrained for the continuous group $\SOtwo$.
Hidden feature maps combined scalar fields $F_0$ and first-order vector fields
$F_1$. The four encoder stages contained 16, 24, 32 and 48 copies of each field
type. Field-aware normalization was used, and each $F_1$ pair shared a radial
gate so that the nonlinearity did not introduce an orientation-dependent
component.

The kernel basis was evaluated on fixed spatial grids and the learned
parameters were the expansion coefficients of that basis. The final posterior
heads produced 16 scalar fields, giving the same
$16\times32\times32$ Gaussian latent shape as the conventional VAE. The
equivariant model contained 1,180,035 learned parameters, or 29.8\% of the
conventional count. The kernel expansion nevertheless increased the estimated
convolutional work, so the lower parameter count should not be interpreted as
lower computational cost.

\subsection{VAE Objective and Training}

Both models were trained as denoising VAEs. With probability 0.3, the input
patch was perturbed in hematoxylin/eosin/residual coordinates using color
deconvolution \cite{ruifrok_color_2001}, and Gaussian noise was added. The
target remained the corresponding clean patch. RGB values were normalized to
$[-1,1]$.

For a clean target $\mathbf{x}$ and reconstruction $\widehat{\mathbf{x}}$, the
objective was

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}={}&\operatorname{MAE}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{x})
+0.1\left[1-\operatorname{SSIM}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{x})\right]\\
&+\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}\mid\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})
\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:vae-loss}
\end{equation}

where SSIM is the structural similarity index \cite{wang_image_2004} and
$\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}$ denotes the corrupted input. The KL weight increased
linearly from zero to $0.01$ during the first 6,000 updates and remained fixed
thereafter.

AdamW \cite{loshchilov_adamw_2019} used coefficients $(0.9,0.999)$,
$\epsilon=10^{-8}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$ and gradient-norm clipping at 1.0.
The learning rate increased linearly for 600 updates to $10^{-3}$ and then
decreased with a cosine schedule to $10^{-5}$. Each model was trained for
60,000 successful updates with a global batch of 50 patches distributed over
two NVIDIA T4 GPUs. Validation and checkpointing occurred every 3,000 updates.
The comparison used the final update-60,000 checkpoint for both models rather
than selecting different VAE checkpoints from the downstream results.

\subsection{Frozen Representations}

After VAE training, all encoder and decoder parameters were frozen. Downstream
tasks used the posterior mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ rather than a sampled latent.
For each selected coordinate, the RGB patch was read once and passed through
both encoders. The two FP32 latent records were stored in the same logical
order, which made WSI membership, tissue labels and patch coordinates
identical between representation branches.

\subsection{Reconstruction Evaluation}

The reconstruction test contained 67,138 foreground patches from the 23 test
WSIs, with at most 3,000 systematically distributed coordinates per slide.
MAE and mean squared error (MSE) were measured in the normalized $[-1,1]$
domain. Peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and SSIM were measured after
conversion to $[0,1]$ and clamping. Mean MAE was the primary reconstruction
endpoint. The other metrics describe complementary error properties.

\subsection{WSI Diagnosis}

The diagnostic task predicted the five ovarian-cancer subtypes. Every selected
foreground patch was represented by its frozen posterior mean. A trainable
convolutional patch encoder compressed the $16\times32\times32$ latent map.
Two local attention blocks exchanged information within a radius-two spatial
neighborhood, and a global stream summarized the complete WSI through learned
sigmoid attention. A final classification layer produced five logits. The
supervised model contained approximately 1.51 million parameters.

Training used one complete WSI bag per optimizer step, AdamW and class-weighted
cross-entropy computed from the 106 training WSIs. Each representation branch
started from the same classifier weights and received the same WSI order. The
maximum was 150 epochs, validation was performed twice per epoch, and each
branch independently retained its highest validation macro-F1 checkpoint.
The primary test metric was macro-F1; balanced accuracy, accuracy, mean
cross-entropy, classwise F1 and confusion matrices were secondary summaries.

\subsection{Tissue Recognition and Label Budgets}

The tissue task predicted tumor, stroma or necrosis from a single frozen latent
map. The classifier contained three convolutional stages
$16\rightarrow32\rightarrow64\rightarrow128$, GroupNorm, GELU activations
\cite{hendrycks_gelu_2016}, adaptive average pooling and a linear
$128\rightarrow3$ output layer.

Training subsets contained 250, 500, 1,000, 2,500 and 5,671 labeled patches
per class. They were class balanced, nested and selected without replacement;
every smaller set was a subset of each larger one. WSI-stratified ordering
ensured that the smallest subset did not collapse onto a single slide. Each
normal/equivariant pair started from identical classifier weights and received
the same patch order. Models were optimized for at most 30 epochs, and the
best validation macro-F1 checkpoint was retained. The test distribution was
not balanced and contained 21,796 tumor, 8,500 stroma and 1,276 necrosis
patches from 23 WSIs.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

All difference intervals are reported as conventional-minus-equivariant.
Reconstruction differences used 10,000 paired cluster-bootstrap replicates at
WSI level \cite{field_bootstrapping_2007}. WSI diagnosis used 10,000 paired
bootstrap replicates stratified by diagnosis. Tissue recognition used a paired
WSI bootstrap stratified by observed tissue support. The five label-budget
macro-F1 differences and the prespecified area under the log-budget learning
curve (AULC) difference form a six-contrast family and use simultaneous 95\%
intervals. Patch-level standard deviations describe patch
dispersion and do not replace WSI-level uncertainty. These procedures estimate
sampling variation across the observed WSIs; they do not measure variation
across new VAE or classifier training seeds.

\subsection{Five-Class WSI Diagnosis}

The equivariant representation obtained higher point estimates for macro-F1

the conventional representation. Its difference interval, $[0.135,0.524]$,

\subsection{Three-Class Tissue Recognition}

The tissue results changed with the amount of labeled training data
(Fig.~\ref{fig:downstream} and Table~\ref{tab:tissue}). The equivariant
representation had higher macro-F1 at 250, 500, 1,000 and 2,500 labels per
class. At 500 labels per class, the macro-F1 values were 0.575 and 0.511, and
the simultaneous interval for conventional-minus-equivariant was
$[-0.121,-0.008]$. This was the only label budget whose simultaneous interval
excluded zero.

At 500 labels per class, the classwise F1 values for
conventional/equivariant representations were 0.769/0.781 for tumor,
0.485/0.560 for stroma and 0.279/0.384 for necrosis. The supported difference
at this budget was therefore not produced by tumor alone.

At the largest budget, the conventional representation obtained macro-F1
0.766 compared with 0.710 for the equivariant representation. The simultaneous
interval included zero, with a lower bound of $-0.001$.
The corresponding classwise F1 values were 0.868/0.871 for tumor,
0.728/0.711 for stroma and 0.701/0.548 for necrosis. The normalized area under
the log-budget learning curve was 0.615 for the conventional representation
and 0.631 for the equivariant representation; its simultaneous difference
interval included zero.

\section{Discussion}

The first research question concerned reconstruction. The conventional VAE had
lower pooled MAE and MSE and higher pooled PSNR and SSIM.
The primary paired MAE interval included zero. The primary analysis therefore
did not establish a reconstruction difference between the two implemented
VAEs.

The second question concerned downstream classification. For WSI diagnosis,
the equivariant representation obtained higher macro-F1 and accuracy, but the
test set contained only 23 slides and the primary difference interval was
wide. The per-class results were also unstable for LGSC and MC, which had two
slides each. These values are point estimates and do not establish a WSI-level
difference.

In tissue recognition, the equivariant representation had higher macro-F1 at
the four smaller budgets. Only the 500-label interval excluded zero; the
remaining budget intervals and the AULC interval included zero. At 5,671 labels
per class, the conventional representation had higher macro-F1, largely because
it had higher necrosis F1. These results do not establish a general increase in
label efficiency.

The two models also differ in capacity and computation. The equivariant VAE
has approximately 30\% of the learned parameters of the conventional VAE, but
its constrained kernel expansion requires more estimated multiply-accumulate
operations. A result from this comparison may arise from equivariance,
parameter count, numerical optimization, or their interaction. The experiment
characterizes the two implemented representations; it does not separate these
mechanisms.

Results differed by endpoint. Reconstruction means and the largest tissue
budget favored the conventional VAE. Selected WSI and smaller-budget tissue
point estimates favored the equivariant representation. The primary WSI
interval and all but one tissue-budget interval included zero, so the experiment
does not establish a general ordering between the representations.

\section{Limitations}

Each VAE and each downstream configuration was represented by one training
trajectory. The bootstrap intervals quantify variation across the observed
WSIs, not variation across new initializations, data orders or optimization
runs. Repeated training would be required to estimate that component.

The WSI test contains 23 slides. LGSC and MC have two slides each, which limits
classwise interpretation and produces wide macro-F1 intervals. Tissue
recognition contains many patches but only 23 independent biological clusters;
necrosis is present in five of them. Patch count therefore overstates the
effective amount of independent test information.

Supplemental tissue masks are incomplete. The tissue experiment evaluates
selected high-purity annotated regions and cannot be interpreted as exhaustive
segmentation of complete WSIs. Results also come from one ovarian-cancer data
source and have not been externally validated on another institution or organ.

The reconstruction aggregation and its bootstrap analysis were finalized
after later test labels had been opened. Neither VAE checkpoint, architecture
nor reconstruction output was changed at that stage, but the statistical
analysis should be regarded as a finalized post-training evaluation rather
than a fully preregistered endpoint.

Finally, the comparison does not match parameter count or computational work.
A future experiment would require repeated seeds, a larger class-complete WSI
test cohort and capacity-matched controls before attributing a classification
difference specifically to continuous rotation equivariance.

\section{Conclusion}

This study compared a conventional convolutional VAE and a continuous-$\SOtwo$
VAE trained on the same histopathology patches and evaluated their frozen
representations in reconstruction, WSI diagnosis and tissue recognition. The
primary reconstruction analysis did not establish a difference between the two
models. The equivariant representation obtained higher point estimates in WSI
macro-F1 and accuracy, but the primary interval included zero. In tissue
recognition, it had higher macro-F1 at four smaller label budgets, with an
interval excluding zero only at 500 labels per class; the conventional
representation had higher macro-F1 at the largest budget. Results varied by
endpoint and did not establish a general advantage for either representation.
